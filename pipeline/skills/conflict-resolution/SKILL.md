# Conflict resolution

Normalize the disagreement to one predicate, one affected operation, and the same version,
configuration and attacker preconditions. Two positions about different paths or deployments may
both be correct within their stated scope. Separate those cases before choosing a side.

Resolve each side's decisive citations independently. Compare what the evidence establishes,
not confidence, repetition, length or the number of agreeing workers. A claimed control must
protect the same operation and state on all relevant paths, including mutation after its check.
An accepted path establishes only the facts its analysis actually proves.

Identify the smallest evidence difference that decides the dispute. If it is unavailable,
preserve disagreement and name that missing fact. If one side overstates impact but identifies
a real predicate failure, distinguish the supported portion from the unsupported consequence.

Summarize the decision per predicate, its resolving citations and residual uncertainty in the
assigned contract. Use anonymous side labels when supplied. Never replace missing proof with
consensus, a scanner rating or absence of a reproduced failure.
